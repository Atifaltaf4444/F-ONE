F1

Formula 1 (F1) is the top class of single-seater auto racing, run by the Formula 1 group and managed by the FIA. Formula 1 (F1) is the highest class of international racing for open-wheel, single-seat formula cars. Competing at speeds exceeding 230 mph (370 km/h), drivers battle across a global circuit known as the Grand Prix.

THE REAL TRUTH

The truth about winning and losing.

Formula 1 winning requires the perfect harmony of elite driving skill, cutting-edge engineering, and flawless team strategy. Drivers must push aerodynamic marvels to their absolute limits, while mechanics execute lightning-fast pit stops.

Formula 1 crashes are high-speed, dramatic events resulting from precise margins of error, mechanical failures, or intense wheel-to-wheel racing. Traveling over 200 mph, these impacts subject drivers to extreme G-forces. Fortunately, advanced safety innovations like the carbon-fiber survival cell, Halo device, and HANS system drastically reduce injuries, keeping modern motorsport remarkably safe.

Formula 1 represents the absolute pinnacle of motorsport, where elite drivers battle at speeds exceeding 200 mph. Twenty racers push cutting-edge, aerodynamic machines to their absolute limits on iconic global circuits. Success demands split-second precision, immense physical endurance, flawless team pit stops, and brilliant strategy to conquer the grid and claim victory.

An F1 trophy represents the pinnacle of motorsport achievement, awarded to top drivers and the winning constructor at each Grand Prix. Crafted from premium materials like carbon fiber, silver, or gold, each trophy features unique designs reflecting the host nation's culture. They stand as enduring symbols of speed, teamwork, and engineering excellence.
